How to play

The green dot is the source. After a countdown — shown as a bar along the bottom of the board — water starts there and moves one square at a time, following whatever openings it finds.

The queue is the game

You don't choose your pieces. Five are visible; you place the first, and a new one joins the back. Getting a straight when you needed a corner is not bad luck, it's the puzzle — the skill is building a route that can absorb whatever arrives.

Building ahead

You can lay pipe anywhere, including squares the water will never reach. Dumping an unusable piece somewhere harmless is a legitimate move and often the right one.

Replacing

Any pipe the water hasn't reached can be overwritten for a small penalty. Once a square is wet it's locked, so mistakes ahead of the flow are recoverable and mistakes behind it are not.

Strategy that actually helps

  • Build a long loop, not a short line. You need a set number of pipes flowed, not a route to anywhere in particular — winding is the point.
  • Keep one square of slack ahead of the water. Building right at the flow leaves no room to react to a bad piece.
  • Dump crosses early. A four-way piece works from any direction, so it's the most flexible thing to have already on the board.
  • Watch the queue, not just the head. If two corners are coming, plan the turn now.
  • Use the countdown. The bar is real building time — spend all of it before hitting Start flow.

Frequently asked questions

How do you play a pipe-laying puzzle?

Water starts at the source after a short countdown and travels one square at a time through whatever pipe you have laid. You place pieces from a queue — you can see what's coming but not reorder it — and you need the flow to reach a set number of pipes before it runs into a dead end.

Can I change a pipe after placing it?

Yes, as long as the water hasn't reached it. Clicking an existing pipe replaces it with the next one from the queue, at a small score penalty. Once a square has water in it, it's fixed.

What happens if the water reaches a dead end?

The level ends. That's the whole tension of the game — you're always building slightly ahead of the flow, and a piece you can't use is a piece you have to dump somewhere harmless.

Does it get harder?

Each level needs a longer run of flowed pipe and gives you less time before the water starts, so you get less building time exactly when you need more.

Do I need to download or sign up for anything?

No. It runs directly in your browser as plain JavaScript — no emulator, no download, no account, and nothing is uploaded.
