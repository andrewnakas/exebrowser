This game, and two more like it

Beneath a Steel Sky (1994)

You are Robert Foster, dragged back to a dystopian city you escaped as a child, with a sarcastic circuit board called Joey for company. Written by Charles Cecil with character and background art by Dave Gibbons, it's one of the best-looking adventure games of the era and routinely appears on best-of lists three decades later. Revolution released it as freeware in 2003, source code and all.

Lure of the Temptress (1992)

Revolution's first game, and the debut of their Virtual Theatre engine — characters wander the town on their own errands whether or not you're watching, which was startling in 1992. You play Diermot, an unremarkable peasant caught up in overthrowing the sorceress Selena. Play it here →

Sołtys

A Polish comedy adventure from L.K. Avalon, released free in 2011 and translated to English. Rarely seen outside Poland, and worth a look precisely because you almost certainly haven't played it. Play it here →

How this works

ScummVM isn't an emulator. It reimplements the engines these games ran on, so instead of simulating a 1994 PC it just runs the game logic directly. That's why it's quick, why it scales cleanly to a modern screen, and why it can run in a browser tab at full speed.

This page runs ScummVM itself compiled to WebAssembly using the project's own Emscripten backend. The engine is GPL-3.0; the games are freeware releases from their rights holders. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing needs installing.

Saves

Your progress is stored in the browser, so you can close the tab and come back. Saves live on this device in this browser — they won't follow you elsewhere.

Frequently asked questions

Is this legal? How can you host these games for free?

Yes. All three games were released as freeware by the companies that own them. Revolution Software released Beneath a Steel Sky in August 2003 — specifically for use with ScummVM, source code included — and Lure of the Temptress in April 2003. L.K. Avalon released Sołtys in November 2011. The files come from ScummVM's own distribution, which is the channel those companies chose. Full details in our provenance notice.

What is ScummVM?

ScummVM is a program that replaces the executables of old adventure games, letting them run on modern hardware. It doesn't emulate a PC — it reimplements the game engines directly, which is why it's fast and why it runs in a browser at full speed. This is a WebAssembly build of ScummVM itself, not an emulator running a Windows binary.

How do I start the game?

You don't — this page loads ScummVM straight into Beneath a Steel Sky and the intro begins on its own. Press Esc to skip it. The other two freeware adventures have their own pages: Lure of the Temptress and Sołtys.

Can I save my progress?

Yes. Saves are kept in your browser's storage, so they survive closing the tab. They're tied to this browser on this device, so they won't follow you to another computer.

Can I add my own games?

Not on this page. ScummVM supports hundreds of adventure games, but we only host the ones that are free to redistribute. If you own others, the desktop version of ScummVM will run them.
