Play chess against the computer ▶ Play now — Stockfish runs in your browser

Guide updated October 2026 · Runs via WebAssembly (native port)

A chess board and an opponent that never gets tired of you. The computer is Stockfish 19, the strongest open-source chess engine there is, compiled to WebAssembly and running inside this page. You choose how hard it plays: level 1 picks loose moves and looks only one move ahead, level 8 thinks for a second and a half at full strength. Drag a piece or tap it and then a square; the board shows where it can go.

Choosing a level

The eight levels combine Stockfish's own Skill Level setting, which makes it deliberately choose weaker moves, with a cap on how far ahead it searches. Roughly:

  • 1–2 — for learning the moves. It leaves pieces hanging and misses simple captures.
  • 3–4 — casual play. It punishes blunders but makes some of its own.
  • 5–6 — a good club player. Expect to lose material if you leave a tactic on the board.
  • 7–8 — very strong. Level 8 is full Stockfish with a short think; a draw against it is an achievement.

You can change the level mid-game; the next computer move uses the new setting.

Playing a game

Pick White, Black or Random and press New game. All the rules are enforced: castling, en passant, promotion (a picker offers queen, rook, bishop or knight), and every draw — stalemate, threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule and insufficient material. Undo takes back your last move and the computer's reply. The move list beside the board uses standard algebraic notation, so you can copy a game into any analysis tool afterwards.

If you close the tab mid-game, the position is kept in this browser and comes back next time you open the page.

How it works

Nothing is sent to a server. The engine is the single-threaded "lite" WebAssembly build of Stockfish 19 from the stockfish.js project, about 1.8 MB including its neural-network evaluation, and it runs in a background worker so the board stays responsive while it thinks. The board itself is chessground, the open-source board that lichess.org uses, and the rules come from chess.js. Because both Stockfish and chessground are GPL-licensed, the code of this page is released under the GPL too; it is served unminified at /apps/chess/.

Frequently asked questions

How strong is the computer at the highest level?

Level 8 is Stockfish 19 at full strength with about 1.5 seconds per move. The lite network is a little weaker than desktop Stockfish, but it still plays far above any human. Lower levels are deliberately weakened with Stockfish's Skill Level option and a shallow search.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up and nothing is uploaded; the engine runs in your browser. Your unfinished game is saved in this browser's local storage.

Can I play on my phone?

Yes. Tap a piece and then the square you want; the board resizes to the screen. The engine is single-threaded, so it works on any modern phone browser without special settings.

Can I play against a friend?

Not on this page yet — it is one player against the computer. You can choose either colour and switch sides between games.
