How to play 2048

The board is four by four. Every move slides every tile as far as it can go in that direction, and two tiles showing the same number merge into their sum. After each move a new 2 — occasionally a 4 — appears in a random empty square.

The merge rule that matters

A tile created by a merge cannot merge again on the same move. That is why a row of four 2s becomes two 4s rather than a single 8, and it is the rule that makes the board fill up.

Winning, and after

Reaching a 2048 tile wins. The game keeps going afterwards, because the real score is in what you build past it.

Strategy that actually helps

  • Pick a corner and never leave it. Keep your largest tile pinned in one corner for the whole game.
  • Use only three directions. If your big tile lives bottom-left, avoid moving right — one careless move dislodges everything.
  • Build a descending row. Along the edge holding your big tile, aim for a run that steps down: 512, 256, 128, 64.
  • Keep the board flat. Lots of small scattered tiles is the state you cannot recover from.
  • Undo the genuinely bad move. One level of undo exists for the move you knew was wrong as you made it.

Frequently asked questions

How do you play 2048?

Every move slides all the tiles as far as they will go. When two tiles with the same number touch, they merge into their sum. A new 2 or 4 appears after each move. Keep merging until you make a 2048 tile.

What is the best strategy?

Pick a corner and keep your biggest tile there, then build a descending row along one edge. Never move in the direction that would pull your big tile out of its corner — that is the single most common way a good board falls apart.

Can you keep playing after 2048?

Yes. Reaching 2048 wins, but the game continues so you can chase 4096 and beyond. Your best score is kept in this browser.

Is this the original 2048?

No. The original was written by Gabriele Cirulli in 2014 and released under the MIT licence. This is a separate implementation written from scratch for ExeBrowser — it shares no code, only the rules, which aren't copyrightable.

Do I need to download or sign up for anything?

No. It runs directly in your browser as plain JavaScript — no emulator, no download, no account, and nothing is uploaded.
