Guide updated November 2026 · Runs via DOSBox + WebAssembly

Death Rally is the 1996 top-down combat racer that was Remedy Entertainment's first game, published by Apogee. You start with a beaten-up Vagabond, race for prize money, and spend it on engines, tyres, armour and less sporting things. How it works & what to expect

The original DOS shareware release runs inside a WebAssembly build of DOSBox. The package is about 7 MB. It opens on the main menu in high-resolution VGA; choose Start Racing and pick a driver.

Races run smoothly on a normal laptop. The menus are 640×480, so they look sharper than most DOS games of the time.

Playing it

Steer with the arrow keys: up accelerates, down brakes. The remaining keys — turbo, machine gun, mines — are shown and can be changed under Configure on the main menu. Early on, enter the easy race, finish in the top three and put the money into the engine before anything else.

Between races the shop sells repairs and upgrades, and the loan shark and the saboteur offer shortcuts that cost more than they first appear to.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal to play Death Rally here?

Yes. This is the official shareware version, licensed by Apogee for free distribution in its LICENSE.DOC. The registered game is not hosted.

What is missing compared with the full game?

The shareware has fewer tracks and cars than the registered game. The racing, weapons, shop and career ladder all work.

Does my career save?

Yes, best-effort. The game saves your driver between races, and this page keeps those files in your browser for your next visit.

Can two people play?

The multiplayer option in the menu needs a DOS network, which a browser tab cannot provide. Single-player is the mode that works here.
