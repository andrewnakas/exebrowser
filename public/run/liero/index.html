Guide updated November 2026 · Runs via DOSBox + WebAssembly

Liero is the 1998 freeware game by Joosa Riekkinen that is often described as real-time Worms: two worms dig through destructible dirt and go at each other with a ninja rope and around forty weapons, with no turns and no waiting. How it works & what to expect

The original DOS game runs inside a WebAssembly build of DOSBox. It is under 400 KB and starts in a second or two; press any key at the loading text to reach the menu.

Liero is light enough to run at full speed on any device.

Playing it

Liero was made for two people sharing one keyboard, and that still works here. You can also set the second worm to be computer-controlled in the options. Each player picks five weapons before the match. The options menu shows each player's keys and lets you change them — do that first, since two sets of hands on one laptop keyboard need some planning.

The ninja rope is the skill that separates players: fire it, swing, and let go.

Why it is free

Liero is freeware. Its documentation says so directly: it may be distributed to anyone, by any means, as long as its contents are not changed. The files here are the unmodified 1.33 release.

Frequently asked questions

Is Liero really free?

Yes. Its author released it as freeware, and the included documentation permits distributing it unchanged. This is the complete game, not a demo.

Can I play alone?

Yes. Set one of the two worms to computer control in the options menu.

How do two people play on one keyboard?

Each player has their own set of keys, shown in the options menu, where you can also change them. A full-size keyboard is more comfortable than a small laptop one.
