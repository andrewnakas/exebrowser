Zork II: The Wizard of Frobozz is Infocom's 1981 text adventure. It picks up where the first game ends, deeper in the Great Underground Empire, where a senile but dangerous wizard appears at random to cast spells on you. In November 2025 Microsoft released the Zork trilogy as open source, and the game runs right here: type a command and press Enter.

How to play

There are no graphics. The game describes where you are, and you type what you want to do: open mailbox, go north (or just n), take lamp, inventory (or i), look. The parser understands full sentences such as put the sword in the case. You begin with the lamp and sword from the first game. The Wizard's spells wear off; waiting is sometimes the right move.

Draw a map as you go. That was the advice in 1980 and it has not changed.

Saving your game

Type save to store your position and restore to return to it. Saved games are kept in this browser, so they will be there on your next visit unless you clear the site's data. The interpreter also keeps an automatic save, so closing the tab does not lose your place.

Why it is free

On 20 November 2025 Microsoft, which owns Infocom's catalogue through Activision, published Zork I, II and III under the MIT licence. Yes. Microsoft released Zork I, II and III under the MIT licence on 20 November 2025. This page runs the game file from that release.

The original. It is Infocom's own compiled story file, run by a modern Z-machine interpreter in your browser.

How do I save?

Type save. Your saved game is stored in this browser; type restore to load it.

Do I need to play the trilogy in order?

Each game stands alone, but they were written as a series and Zork I is the best place to start.
