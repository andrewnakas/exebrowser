How it works & what to expect

The original DOS release (version 1.3D) running in a WebAssembly build of DOSBox, set to give it as much CPU as your device has. On a recent laptop or desktop it plays smoothly; a phone or an older Chromebook will be choppier, because every instruction of a game that wanted a fast 486 is being emulated. The package is under 6 MB and is cached after the first load.

Playing tips

Keys open doors and the coloured card readers — the shareware levels hide them behind vents you can kick or shoot open. Hold Shift to run, which you will want almost all the time. The jetpack and steroids in your inventory are selected with [ and ] and used with Enter. Mirrors, toilets and arcade cabinets all react — that is the game, not a bug.

Shareware episode — and the full game

Episode 1 is the shareware episode, and its licence lets free websites offer it — so long as every one of its files ships unmodified, which they do here. Episodes 2 and 3 (and the later Atomic Edition's fourth) were the commercial release and are not included; the full game is still sold.

Frequently asked questions

Is Duke Nukem 3D legal to play here?

Yes. This is the official shareware episode, whose licence explicitly allows free websites to make it available. The commercial episodes are not hosted.

What are the controls?

Arrow keys move, Ctrl fires, Space opens doors and uses switches, A jumps, Z crouches, Shift runs and Tab shows the map. The full table is under the game.

Why is it slow on my phone?

Duke 3D wanted a fast 486 in 1996, and here every one of its instructions is emulated in your browser. A recent desktop or laptop handles it; a phone or a budget Chromebook will drop frames.

Does my progress save?

Yes — saved games persist in this browser between visits, best-effort. Save from the game's own menu with F2.

Can I play the full game here?

Only the shareware episode is hosted. If you own the DOS version, drop its folder into the DOS emulator page and it runs the same way.
